How we rank

Every page here is computed from a named public dataset, and each page shows its formula, its filters and a downloadable table (CSV and JSON) so you can recheck it. Electric cars rank on the EPA combined kWh per 100 miles from the fueleconomy.gov data (95% of the score) with rated range as the small remainder; each model is listed once in its most efficient EPA-rated configuration, and the spread to its least efficient configuration is shown. Home charging cost multiplies that figure by an assumed monthly mileage stated on the page and the EIA average U.S. residential electricity price. Heat pump dryers rank on ENERGY STAR's certified Combined Energy Factor (95%), with the DOE test cycle length as the tie-breaker (5%); yearly kWh is ENERGY STAR's certified estimate, priced at the EIA residential rate for the five-year cost. Compact dryers and models without a heat pump flag in the certification record are left out, and identical models sold under several brand names are merged. The water heater page does not rank products: it compares the yearly running cost of a heat pump, an efficient gas storage heater and an electric resistance heater state by state from EIA average electricity and natural gas prices, shows the break-even electricity price, and lets you enter your own prices.

In every ranking the criteria and weights are shown on the page, each value is scored against the best on the page (lower-is-better numbers as best divided by value), multiplied by its weight and summed to a score out of 100. A page's 'Why #1 is #1' block shows the margin per criterion and what would change the order. Price is not part of any score and we do not list product prices. A page states when its data was retrieved and when it refreshes next.

We did not test any vehicle, dryer or water heater: efficiency figures are EPA label or ENERGY STAR certified figures, and real use differs with climate, driving, laundry habits and hot water use. We do not accept payment for placement.
